Research

Optimal Monetary and Fiscal Policy Rules under Debt Collateralization

Job Market Paper

This paper studies optimal monetary and fiscal policy rules when government debt and capital serve as collateral in financial markets. When borrowing constraints tighten, collateral premiums distort investment and production. The paper examines how monetary policy should respond to these financial conditions and finds that optimal policy actively eases when collateral premiums rise.

Optimal Supply of Government Debt: Collateralization and Overaccumulation of Capital

Working Paper

Capital can substitute for government debt in collateral markets. When the collateral premium is high, firms may overaccumulate capital to relax borrowing constraints. Government debt can crowd out this inefficient capital demand while also creating fiscal costs through debt service and distortionary taxation.

Analysis of Russia's Wartime Economy: Focusing on Fiscal and Monetary Policy

Yong Hoon Cho

KIEP 기초자료 25-06 · December 29, 2025 · [in Korean]

This report divides Russia's wartime economy into three phases: the initial 2022 shock, the 2023–24 overheating period, and the 2025 slowdown. It examines how fiscal expansion, defense spending, interest-rate policy, capital controls, and tax increases were combined to stabilize the ruble and inflation while sustaining wartime demand. The report also argues that a postwar soft landing will require renewed investment because labor shortages and intensive capital utilization have increased the risk that aging equipment becomes a future production bottleneck.

The Impact of the Iran War on the Russian Economy and Policy Implications

Yong Hoon Cho

KIEP 세계경제포커스 26-32 · May 6, 2026 · [in Korean]

This report analyzes how the Iran war and disruption to global energy supply altered Russia's fiscal outlook. Russia entered 2026 under growing budget pressure from weak oil-and-gas revenues and persistent wartime expenditure, but the subsequent rise in global oil prices and narrowing discount on Urals crude temporarily improved fiscal revenues and delayed planned tightening. The report emphasizes that this relief is a short-term buffer, while also noting implications for Korea's energy-security strategy and supply diversification.

Outcomes of the Korea–Mongolia Summit and Policy Implications

Yong Hoon Cho

KIEP 세계경제포커스 26-53 · July 23, 2026 · [in Korean]

This report reviews the July 2026 Korea–Mongolia summit and assesses its economic-policy significance. It highlights the in-principle conclusion of CEPA negotiations and expanded cooperation in critical minerals, energy transition, infrastructure, climate policy, digital technology, and people-to-people exchange. While the summit created a broader institutional foundation for cooperation, the report stresses that many outcomes remain at the MOU stage and that implementation, private investment, and follow-up mechanisms will determine whether they translate into durable economic gains.
